## The problem this project addressed

Stake pool operators are, as a population, technically excellent and commercially invisible.
Most can build and run infrastructure. Far fewer can say what their pool is for, who it serves,
what value it creates, or how it survives. SPO Academy was funded to close that gap with a
business course written for operators, delivered on-chain, with a credential at the end.

**Testing performed.** The course was run live with a real cohort of nine operators across five
sessions. Pool identifiers and Calidus IDs are verified against the Cardano chain at
application. The credential path was exercised end to end, from course creation through to a
participant claim, with the transactions recorded on chain.

**User feedback.** Three written responses to the feedback form, all three consenting to
attribution, plus a verbal round in the final session and continuous feedback in the cohort
Discord. Compiled in the [feedback report](../cohort/feedback-report.md), with every resulting
action owned and dated in the [action plan](../cohort/improvements-and-actions.md).

**Who uses it.** Cardano stake pool operators, mainnet and testnet. The cohort included
established operators, one running five years, and prospective operators without a mainnet pool
yet, which the course was deliberately built to serve.

**Key actions completed by users.** Minting an Andamio access token, working the module
assignments against their own pool, building a plan in the Playbook Builder, claiming an
on-chain credential, and submitting written feedback.

**Evidence of engagement.** Each session has a published write-up and recording. Participants
submitted assignments between sessions, raised platform friction in the cohort channel as it
happened, and two have described continuing to build their plan after the cohort closed.

Completion ran ahead of claiming. Operators did the work and left the credential unclaimed,
which is the same signal as their strongest product ask: a credential is worth claiming when
there is somewhere to show it. That is what the credentials package in the recommendations is
for.

## 3. Impact

**What changed for the operators.** Every form respondent reported an unprompted shift in how
they think about their pool, and all three said the plan they built is one they will use. One
had already started an evolving playbook for their own pool. One stated they will execute on
the plan.

> Previously, it was easy to focus on increasing delegation, producing blocks and maintaining
> reliable infrastructure. Those things remain important, but I'm increasingly viewing
> delegation growth as a possible outcome of creating meaningful value rather than the primary
> reason for operating the pool.
>
> RCADA

Asked in the final session what the course changed for them, the answers were: seeing the gaps in how a
pool measures the value it creates; the difference between a pool's philosophy and the systems
that run it; thinking of a pool as an organism rather than a machine; the pool's financial gaps
and the types of delegator it serves; and, for a five-year operator, putting words to the
personal development the work has brought.

**Before and after.** Before this project there was no business-focused course for Cardano
stake pool operators, and no on-chain credential for one. There is now, with nine operators
through it and the material permanently available and self-paced.

**Benefit to the Cardano ecosystem.** Small pools that can articulate their purpose and sustain
themselves are what keeps the network decentralised. The course also put Andamio's
credentialing through a full live cohort and fed detailed findings back to their team, who
asked for them and acted on them. Every course built on that platform benefits from that
round trip.

**Partnerships and recognition.** Delivered with Edify. The course was presented in person to
the xSPO Alliance weekly call on 4 October 2026, and announced across the Andamio, SPO Call,
xSPO Alliance, RealFi, IOG / Intersect and Midnight communities.

## 4. Sustainability

The project is ongoing, not a one-off.

**Maintenance model.** The course stays open and self-paced on Andamio. Fluid7 maintains the
public site, the application flow and the Playbook Builder. The seventeen improvements in the
[action plan](../cohort/improvements-and-actions.md) each carry an owner and a date.

**Revenue model.** Pricing was adopted on 5 October 2026: $295 for a guided cohort, $210
self-paced, and an operator-set price for pools estimated to earn under 5,000 ADA a year. The
launch cohort was not charged, so these prices are published but untested. The second cohort
has to charge for that to change.

**Future roadmap.** A second cohort once the material is reworked, a dedicated session with
Andamio to get the platform items onto their roadmap, and the credentials package the cohort
asked for. A further live session for this cohort may be arranged once the updated material is
ready.

**On the participant count.** Nine against a target of fifteen is the one criterion missed.
Marketing was not the constraint: fourteen operators gave their contact details, ten through the
persona quiz alone, across fifty-six published campaign items. The constraint was conversion
from interest to commitment. A stake pool operator is usually a
sole trader running infrastructure in the evenings, and a four-session course asking for written
assignments about their own business is a real ask. The nine who committed produced more usable
evidence than fifteen passive enrolments would have.

## Lessons learned

1. **A cohort target is a conversion assumption, not a marketing one.** We reached operators
   comfortably and converted fewer than planned. The next target should be set on how many
   operators will commit to assignments, not on how many will give an email address.
2. **Choosing a platform is choosing a partner, and the partner matters more than the feature
   list.** Andamio gave us on-chain credentials we could not have built within the budget.
   Participants still hit real friction: submission costs the platform did not surface, a module
   that stuck in assessment, no way to resume at the next module. What made that workable was
   Andamio's response. They asked for the detail, took the reports as they came, and shipped
   fixes while the cohort was still running. The remaining items are with them, and a dedicated
   session is agreed to work them into the roadmap. Friction is inevitable on any platform. A
   team that answers is not, and it is the thing to assess before you commit.
3. **A self-paced course with a live series runs on two clocks.** The course is deliberately
   asynchronous: the material stands alone and participants set their own pace, which is the
   right design for operators fitting study around running infrastructure. The four live
   sessions ran to a fixed calendar, which implied a pace the course does not actually impose.
   One participant worked four modules in the final week to meet a deadline that was never
   really there. The answer is signposting rather than scheduling: say which modules a session
   will draw on, and let the operator decide whether to be ahead of it.
4. **Written feedback under-reports.** Three of nine responded in writing. The verbal round in
   the final session surfaced four themes the form missed entirely, including the strongest
   product ask of the project, a credentials package operators can show to funders.
5. **Material written to teach a framework grows past what the work needs.** Two respondents
   independently said the modules carry more words than the assignments require, and one said it
   read as machine-written. Length is not rigour.
6. **A free launch cohort gives no price signal.** Nothing was charged, so the published prices
   remain untested.

## Recommendations for future development

Before a second cohort:

1. Build one complete worked SPO business model, end to end, and make it the spine of the course
   rather than an appendix. This was the clearest single ask.
2. Cut the overlapping frameworks. Teach one model per idea and cross-reference the rest.
3. Signpost rather than schedule. Say which modules each live session draws on, so a
   participant can choose where to be without the course turning into a timetable.
4. Restructure the live hour as application, not re-presentation, and give it a written
   participation route for operators who would rather not speak.
5. Clear the production debt: audio quality, narration pace, contrast in module 503, image
   sizing in module 100, and hyperlinked cross-references.

Beyond that:

6. **Build the credentials package.** The cohort asked for a logbook they can put in front of
   partners and funders. A per-module credential nobody outside the platform can read is worth
   less than the same credential in a document an operator can send.
7. **Charge the second cohort**, to test the pricing adopted in October.
8. **Work the platform feedback into Andamio's roadmap** in a dedicated session rather than as
   individual tickets.

## Final thoughts

The course works. Operators who finished it think differently about their pools and have a plan
they say they will use, which is the outcome the proposal was funded for. What this cohort
proved is that the hard part is not teaching operators business, it is getting them to commit
the hours. That is a product problem, and it has answers: a worked example instead of theory, a
pace someone can follow, and a credential worth showing to someone else.
