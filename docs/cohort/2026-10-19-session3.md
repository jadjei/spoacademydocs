## What was covered

**Market Biome and Internal Biome**: who your delegators actually are and which ones you want,
and what your pool is made of.

The biomes are three ways of looking at your environment. The external one used to be the slow
one: politics, economics, technology, law, environment and society, the classic PESTLE, which
shifted over years. Exponential technologies now wrap around all of it, so change that took
years now takes months or less. Most people sit outside that system. Stake pool operators sit
inside it, which is both the difficulty and the advantage.

Two ideas carried the session:

1. **Most of the value you create does not show on a balance sheet.** GDP and conventional
   accounts measure what is produced, not whether it is worth producing, and they miss most
   intangible value: relationships, know-how, community, trust. A pool's strongest asset is its
   relationship with its delegators, and that is also what makes a pool attractive to projects
   that want a real connection into a decentralised community.
2. **Find your engine.** A value chain works like a theatre: someone writes the play, the cast
   rehearse and perform it, someone fills the seats, and someone runs the building. Each adds
   value differently. Decide which part you do best, whether that is running reliable
   infrastructure, building a delegator community or something else, focus there, and bring in
   channel partners to reach people you could not reach alone.

## What was discussed

- Running a pool on its block rewards alone is hard to sustain at current costs. The operators
  doing well tend to have other activity around the pool that brings visibility and income.
- Protocol parameters, rather than delegator education alone, may decide long-term pool
  sustainability. Proposals are under discussion for future hard forks.
- Pools as collaborators rather than competitors. One example from the ecosystem: a Discord
  tool whose operators pay for it by delegating to the developer's pool, so the stake stays
  with its owner and the tool's growth and the delegation grow together. The open question was
  whether the people who use such a tool are the people inclined to delegate.
- AI has removed much of the technical barrier to running a pool, which shifts the advantage
  further towards relationships, service and trust.
- Crypto still reads as "scam" to much of the public. A suggested route in is a useful service
  first, with the blockchain underneath discovered later.
- The tribalism between and within ecosystems, and whether interoperability work can soften it.
- Revisiting the course after a few years was described as worthwhile: the exercises are meant
  to be redone as the ecosystem changes, not completed once.
